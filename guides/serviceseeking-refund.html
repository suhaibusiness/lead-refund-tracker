ServiceSeeking refunds

Can you get a refund from ServiceSeeking?

Usually not. ServiceSeeking sells memberships, not leads, so there’s no lead credit to claim. Its terms say payments aren’t refundable and unused prepaid time isn’t refunded, “subject to Australian Consumer Law”. You can stop the next payment at any time.

What you pay for

  • A ServiceSeeking membership lets you contact and quote customers whose jobs match your industry and area settings.
  • You pay for the membership, not for each lead, so a bad lead or a customer who never answers has no credit of its own to claim.
  • That’s why the number of jobs you can quote on matters more than any single lead.

Stopping the next payment

  • Log in, open My Membership (your name, top right on a computer; the Menu icon on a phone), click Cancel and pick a reason.
  • You keep the membership until the expiry date shown there.
  • If the online cancel won’t go through, email support@serviceseeking.com.au and say clearly you want to cancel. It must arrive at least 2 business days before the next billing date.
  • Keep a screenshot or the email as proof of the date you cancelled.

“…Paying businesses can cancel future payments for their membership at any time…”

ServiceSeeking Terms & Conditions: Cancellations of Paid Business Memberships, read 12 November 2026

What the refund terms say

  • Payments are non-refundable when made, subject to Australian Consumer Law.
  • Prepaid time you don’t use isn’t refunded.
  • Anything the policy doesn’t cover is at ServiceSeeking’s discretion. Refunds it does give are paid within 60 business days, back the way you paid.
  • Don’t go to your bank first. The terms say a chargeback without contacting support first suspends the membership, and ServiceSeeking may invoice the amount back, use a debt collector and add costs.

“…will not be refunded for the unused portion of their membership…”

ServiceSeeking Terms & Conditions, read 12 November 2026

Where Australian Consumer Law still applies

  • The ACCC says a business buying a service is covered by the consumer guarantees when it costs less than $100,000 including GST.
  • One guarantee is that services are provided with due care and skill, are fit for purpose, and arrive within a reasonable time.
  • When a service fails a guarantee, the ACCC says the buyer is entitled to a remedy, which can be a refund. Which remedy depends on whether the problem is major or minor.
  • The ACCC also says a business can’t take away those rights by putting up a sign or saying “no refunds”.

Whether a membership that brought almost no matching jobs fails a guarantee depends on the facts. Put your case to ServiceSeeking in writing first, with your numbers.

“…Services must be provided with due care and skill…”

ACCC: Small business rights and responsibilities, read 12 November 2026

Example

A written request (change it to yours):

Account: Your Business, office@yourbusiness.com

I paid $[amount] for a 12-month membership on [date]. In [number] months I received [number] job invitations matching my trade and area, and none could be quoted because [reason]. I’m asking for a refund of the unused part under the consumer guarantees in the Australian Consumer Law. My records of each invitation are attached.

The catch. The money question is decided by your own record: how many matching jobs came in each month, and what each cost you. Without dates and numbers, a refund request is an opinion. With them, it’s a case.
