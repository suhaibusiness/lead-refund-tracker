Google Local Services Ads lead credits: how to get money back for a bad lead in 2026

There’s no claim to file: Google credits poor leads by itself. It keeps checking each charged lead for 30 days, and credits usually reach your balance within 30 days. What you can do is check the lead should have been charged at all, rate it, and tighten your settings.

“Google continuously assesses lead quality over a 30-day period.”

Calls that shouldn’t be charged at all

  • A missed call where the caller hung up within 20 seconds.
  • A missed call outside your business hours (the hours in your Google Business Profile) or outside your ad schedule.
  • A caller who hung up at your phone menu without pressing a key. The 20 seconds start from the first key press.
  • A follow-up call or message from the same customer, from the same number or email, within 15 days of your last contact.
  • Spam and duplicate contacts, which Google filters out before charging.

A call that reaches voicemail during business hours is charged if the caller stays on for more than 20 seconds, message or not. Each lead shows its Charge Status in Google Ads: Charged, Not charged, In review or Credited.

What Google credits, and what it stopped crediting

Google’s models reassess charged leads and credit the ones they judge low quality, duplicate or invalid. Leads for a job type or an area you don’t serve aren’t credited any more, so fix those at the source: tighten your service areas, job types and categories in your settings. Expect fewer leads when you do.

“Google no longer supports credits for “job type not serviced” and “geo not serviced” leads.”

Rate the lead

  1. Sign in to your Local Services Ads account.
  2. Open the menu in the top left and select Leads.
  3. Select Rate this lead in the top right corner, open the survey and submit your feedback.

Google says it may occasionally credit a lead you report as poor quality, and a credit can also come as a bonus for leaving feedback. Either way, the original charge still shows on your invoice.

“In most cases, credits will be applied to your account balance within 30 days.”

Who gets no credits at all

  • Health care businesses.
  • Tax specialists.
  • Advertisers in Europe, the Middle East and Africa.

The catch. Owners report being charged for sales calls and spam that got through. With no claim form, rating the lead is the only lever you have, so rate every bad one and keep your own record of what you were charged and what came back.
