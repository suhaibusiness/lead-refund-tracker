Yelp Ads refunds

Can you get a refund from Yelp Ads for bad clicks or leads?

Mostly no. Yelp Ads charge per click, not per lead, so there’s no claim for a bad lead the way Angi, Thumbtack or Bark have. Yelp filters some invalid clicks before billing, and its ad terms make other people’s fake clicks your risk.

What you pay for

  • Yelp Ads are cost per click: you pay each time someone clicks your ad, whether or not they ever contact you.
  • The price per click moves with demand, so daily spend varies, but Yelp says it never charges more than the monthly budget you set.
  • So a time-waster who calls, or a lead who never answers, isn’t something you were charged for as a lead, and there’s no lead to claim back.

“Each time your ad is clicked, we will charge you according to your CPC price.”

Yelp Business Help: How does Yelp’s CPC advertising program work?, read 12 November 2026

The clicks Yelp says it doesn’t bill

  • Yelp says it watches clicks and impressions for fraud and filters known offenders.
  • Repeated clicks from the same person in quick succession aren’t billed.
  • Yelp says it investigates reports of click fraud from advertisers.

“…we do not bill for clicks that come in rapid succession from the same user…”

Yelp Business Help, on click fraud, read 12 November 2026

What the ad terms say

  • Other people’s clicks: fake or accidental clicks by third parties are your risk. Where the law doesn’t allow that, the most Yelp gives is replacement advertising, not money.
  • Yelp’s own invalid clicks: if Yelp itself causes them, the remedy is again replacement advertising equal to those clicks.

These are Yelp’s US Master Advertising Terms dated 11 May 2026. Your own purchase order wins where it differs, so check it.

“CLIENT ACCEPTS THE RISK OF THIRD-PARTY ACTIVITY WITHOUT LIABILITY TO YELP.”

Yelp Master Advertising Terms (11 May 2026), read 5 November 2026

What you can do

  • If you see clicks you believe are fraudulent, report them to Yelp: (877) 767-9357, or log in to the Business Help Center.
  • Keep your own record: each Yelp click or call, the date, and whether it was a real customer. Without it you can’t show Yelp a pattern, or judge whether the ads pay.
  • Compare that record with your monthly spend before you renew.

Example

A click-fraud report (change it to yours):

Business: Your Business, account email office@yourbusiness.com

Between 3 and 9 Nov my ad was clicked 41 times, and 0 of those led to a message or call. 17 clicks came between 2:00 and 2:20am on 6 Nov. Please check these for invalid activity.

The catch. Owners often compare Yelp with Angi or Thumbtack and expect a lead credit. Yelp doesn’t sell leads, so the only money question is whether the clicks are real. Your own log of clicks against real enquiries is the one thing that answers it.
